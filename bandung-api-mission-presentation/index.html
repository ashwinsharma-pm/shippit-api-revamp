02 / Behaviour changes

Delta 4 Framework

New−Old≥4

Score the old way and the new way for efficiency, 1 to 10. Subtract. Clear four and three things follow.

01

Behaviour becomes irreversible

02

Users forgive your mistakes

03

Users brag about it

Kunal Shah · The Knowledge Project, ep. 141
Before the example, the frame. Kunal Shah scores the old way of doing a job and the new way, each out of ten for efficiency, and subtracts. When the gap is four or more, three things happen. Behaviour becomes irreversible: nobody returns to the old method. Users tolerate mistakes: parts of the new product can be broken and they still stay. And users brag: the product earns a Unique Brag-worthy Proposition, and word of mouth starts to matter more than the selling proposition. Keep those three in mind. They are the test for whether a software-operated path is worth building.

03 / Delta 4 in practice

A better interface can make the old one feel impossible.

Illustrative example. Screenshot: Uber app, uber.com.
Now the example everyone knows. Hailing a taxi on the street scores about three out of ten. Requesting an Uber scores about nine. The delta is six, well past four, and all three effects showed up: nobody went back to the kerb, people tolerated surge pricing and cancelled drivers, and Uber spread by word of mouth. The scores are illustrative. The point is that an interface can reset expectations for a whole category. Once you can ask software to finish a job, a click path can begin to feel like standing on the street waiting for a cab.

04 / Delta 4 in practice
Second example, closer to home. On 30 November 2022 Sam Altman posted one line: today we launched ChatGPT, try talking with it. Score the old way of getting an answer from software, a search box and ten blue links, and score asking a question in plain language and getting the answer back. The delta cleared four. All three effects followed: people did not go back, they tolerated hallucinations and outages, and they showed it to everyone they knew. That is the behaviour shift we are designing for. The people who work with Shippit have already had their Delta 4 moment with conversational software.

05 / Delta 4 in practice
And here is the reply underneath that tweet. A reasonable, informed person looked at the same launch and called it the worst product concept so far. He was scoring the technology as it stood: hallucinations, no memory, no tools. Users were scoring something else, the gap between typing a question and getting an answer versus everything they had done before. That gap cleared four, so the flaws were forgiven. Remember this when we look at agents operating Shippit. The current tech will be criticised, correctly, on many points. The question that predicts behaviour is the delta, not the defect list.

06 / The usage model

Two parties talked to Shippit. A third has arrived.

For years there were two parties on the customer side. A person opened Shippit and clicked. An engineer wrote code against the API once and left it running. Click. A third party has arrived: software that reads context, chooses a tool, acts, and checks the result, on the customer's behalf and without a fixed integration. Humans remain accountable. What changes is how much of the path software operates. API Mission exists because this third party is far less tolerant of ambiguity than a person clicking around a screen.

07 / Agents, plainly

An agent keeps going until the job is done

To an agent, the tool path is the product.

An agent is not just a chatbot. It reads context, chooses a tool, acts, checks the result, and keeps going. That loop is familiar engineering. The important difference is that the software may invent a path when we have not provided one. If our contract is incomplete or inconsistent, the agent does not politely wait for the next quarter. It scrapes, guesses, asks a human, or fails.

Directional · illustrative · not measured Shippit volume

The recognisable human audience does not disappear. It becomes the visible speck beside a much larger software audience. That scale changes the cost of ambiguity, weak rate limits, inconsistent errors, and unclear identity.

15 / The thesis
MCP discovers.APIs execute.
This is the simplest way to remember the division of labour. MCP helps an agent find and call the available tools. The API remains the typed contract that executes the job. Identity determines who can do what, for which tenant, with what authority, and with what audit trail. MCP does not replace the API and it is not the first SKU. It depends on the jobs and the trust layer beneath it.

24 / Adoption is product work

Adoption will be a big challenge.

Phase 01 / Decide

Define the scope and the cohort.

  • First workflows and segments.
  • Migration boundary and success measure.
Phase 02 / Prove

Build the safe first wins.

  • Sandbox, fixtures, validators.
  • Identity checks and lifecycle states.
Phase 03 / Move

Make migration a program.

  • Named cohorts with playbooks.
  • Incentives, deadlines, fallback rules.
Phase 04 / Govern

Make change a discipline.

  • Lifecycle policy and version windows.
  • Deprecation rules and adoption telemetry.
Adoption is product work. Decide, prove, move, govern. Waiting for customer permission is not a plan.
